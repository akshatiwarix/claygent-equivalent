Know why you're emailing before you write the email.

Enter a company domain. In about a minute you get a one-page brief: what they sell, what changed in the last 90 days, and three cold-email openers. Every fact links to its source and shows the sentence that backs it up.

One live run per visitor per day. Or open a saved example:

Two model calls, then a check

Research

A model searches the web, at most five times, and writes notes with a date, a link and an exact quote for every event.

Write

A second call, with no web access, turns only those notes into three pains and three openers in a fixed format.

Check

Is each event from the last 90 days? Was its link returned by the search? Is the quote on the page, and does the page text back up the claim? Events that fail are dropped, or trimmed to what the source says, and the pains built on them are rewritten. Dead links and generic openers are fixed automatically. Everything removed is listed.

Spot-checked by hand on an earlier version: 34 of 99 facts across 10 briefs, 0 wrong. Saved examples cost $0.045 each on average and took about 75 seconds. A small sample, not proof of zero errors.

Briefs are generated from public web sources and can contain errors. Check the linked sources before you send anything.

Part of a GTM stack: briefs feed an enrichment pipeline and, next, a signal engine that watches for the events behind them.
